TrackEverything:
Dense 3D Point Tracking in Long Videos via 3D Scene Representations

Ayush Jain1,2, Sreeharsha Paruchuri1, Ishita Gupta1, Fan Zhang2,
Tanner Schmidt2, Jakob Engel2, Katerina Fragkiadaki1, Adam W. Harley2

1Carnegie Mellon University 2Meta

Paper arXiv Code

TrackEverything tracks all points across all frames of videos with 1000+ frames by representing the video as persistent 3D scene tracks in world coordinates.

It outperforms open-source dense 3D trackers by over 20% APD on TAPVid-3D while staying under 40 GB of peak GPU memory with only 61M parameters.

Abstract

Videos are 2D projections of a persistent 3D world, yet most tracking methods operate in pixel space, redundantly re-representing the same surfaces across frames. We introduce TrackEverything, a dense 3D point tracker that represents videos as persistent 3D scene tracks in world coordinates. Our approach combines three ideas: (1) tracking in 3D scene-content space, where repeated observations of the same surface are fused and occluded points persist without redundant representation; (2) a recurrent architecture that iteratively refines motion estimates and chains predictions across sliding windows for long-video inference; and (3) per-window static/dynamic classification that allocates trajectory decoding only to points in motion. At each window boundary, the 3D representation is voxelized to remove redundant points, ensuring model complexity scales with distinct scene content rather than video length. The result is the first dense 3D point tracker that tracks all points across all frames in videos of 1000+ frames, outperforming open-source dense 3D trackers by over 20% APD on TAPVid-3D while running in under 40 GB of GPU memory.

Method

Architecture overview: a 3D feature cloud is refined by an EndPoint Refiner and decoded into dense trajectories only where the scene moves.

Failure Cases

Tracking degrades under fast, large-displacement motion and under repetitive or low-texture backgrounds, where the lifted 3D correspondences become ambiguous.
